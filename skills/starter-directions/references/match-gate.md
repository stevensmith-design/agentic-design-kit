# Named-starter match — the gate

Runs only when a starter has been **named** against a direction someone supplied — by the person,
or because they asked whether one fits. Open that one starter's file and nothing else in the
catalogue. Never run this to *find* a starter for a supplied direction: searching is the anchoring
this gate exists to stop.

## Five dimensions

1. **Composition** — the page's structure and how the first screen is spent.
2. **Type roles** — display to text to label relationships and their hierarchy (not font names).
3. **Surface and colour behaviour** — ground band, how surfaces separate, what colour does and how
   much of it there is.
4. **Object family** — the illustration, apparatus or interface objects the page is built from.
5. **Motion and interaction grammar** — what moves, why, on what trigger.

The starter may be used only when the supplied direction matches on **four of five**. A shared
palette, a general mood or generous whitespace is resemblance, not a match.

Then: the non-matching dimension and what governs it instead (always the supplied direction); the
**boundary** — exactly what the starter may inform and what it may not; and a check that the
starter conflicts with no explicit exclusion or content need in the direction. The supplied
direction overrides the starter wherever they differ.

Fails the gate → say so in one line and hand back to `/design-build` as reference-informed or
system-led. Passes → the route is **named-starter match**; the stamp records the starter, the
boundary in one line, and distance suspended.
